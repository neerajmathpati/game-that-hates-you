Product summary and product thesisThe Game That Hates You is a 5-10 minute browser-based rage platformer where the difficulty adapts to the individual player. Instead of generating random unfairness, the game observes repeatable behavior, forms lightweight behavior hypotheses, and selects future challenges that counter those behaviors. The emotional arc is intentionally designed: normal -&gt; suspicious -&gt; realization -&gt; personal challenge -&gt; unpredictability.One-line pitch:  A rage game that learns how you play - and then uses your own habits against you.The central product mechanic is not the trap itself. It is the recognition moment when the player understands that the game has been watching their repeated choices. The experience must therefore make adaptations observable without exposing the full system too early.Product promiseFeels normal first.  The opening teaches movement and basic interaction without immediately revealing adaptation.Learns behavior.  The game tracks directional bias, jump frequency, hesitation, route preference, retry patterns, and other high-signal actions.Counters habits.  Later levels deliberately place avoidable danger where the player is overusing a strategy.Explains the joke.  After selected failures, the game reveals a small observation such as “You chose left 8 times.”Ends personally.  The final level combines several learned traits and can only be solved by changing the player’s established pattern.2. Goals and non-goalsGoalsCreate a memorable 5-10 minute playable experience with a clear beginning, escalation, reveal and ending.Demonstrate genuine player-adaptive difficulty using deterministic, inspectable logic rather than random punishment.Collect enough behavior evidence to make the player feel understood without storing personally identifying data.Keep the technical scope small enough for rapid development and Vercel deployment.Make the full prompt/development sequence easy to document for the competition submission.Non-goalsNo accounts, multiplayer, matchmaking, leaderboard or social graph in MVP.No large procedurally generated world; levels are hand-authored templates with adaptive modifiers.No mandatory runtime LLM call; the core game cannot depend on network availability.No real-world behavioral profiling beyond the current game session.No attempt to infer sensitive personal traits; only game actions and timing are recorded.3. Target player and experience principlesPrimary audience: players who enjoy rage games, short indie browser games, novelty mechanics and streamer-friendly “did the game just read my mind?” moments. The design should also work for a first-time browser player with no prior explanation.Experience principlesReadable before difficult.  Every major trap must have visual or timing cues that make the failure understandable after one or two attempts.Adaptive, not arbitrary.  The player should be able to connect the challenge to a repeated behavior.Counterable.  Every adaptive challenge must have at least one viable counter-strategy.Reveal gradually.  Observation messages become more explicit as confidence in the behavior model grows.Fast retry.  Death-to-retry should feel nearly instant so the player experiments instead of disengaging.4. Adaptive behavior system requirementsThe MVP uses behavior features and confidence scores. “AI” here means an adaptive decision system: it learns from interaction data, updates hypotheses and chooses the next challenge. This keeps the game deterministic, fast and deployable without an external AI dependency.Tracked signalsSignalDefinitionWhy it mattersMinimum captureDirection biasShare of meaningful movement time or choices left vs right.Detects preferred route/direction.Per attempt.Jump frequencyJumps per 10 seconds and jumps near hazards.Detects jump-first strategy.Rolling window.HesitationTime between entering a decision zone and committing.Detects cautious vs impulsive play.Per decision.Rush scoreMovement velocity and decision speed before hazards.Separates rushers from cautious players.Per segment.Explore scoreOptional path/side-object interactions.Detects curiosity/exploration.Per attempt.Retry patternRepeated same action sequence after death.Detects strategy persistence.Across attempts.Reaction to deathImmediate retry, pause, route change, etc.Allows meta-level personality responses.After death.Behavior hypothesesLEFT_BIASED / RIGHT_BIASED: direction share &gt; 0.68 across at least 2 decision pointsJUMP_HEAVY: jump rate &gt; threshold and jumps near hazardsRUSHER: median hesitation below threshold and high movement speedCAUTIOUS: median hesitation above threshold and repeated stopping zonesREPETITIVE: same route/action sequence repeated &gt;= 3 timesEXPLORER: high optional-interaction rate or side-path rateAdaptation constraintsNever make a counter unavoidable solely because of the learned habit.Never stack more than two high-intensity counters in the same micro-section until Level 4.At least one safe or recoverable response must remain visible or discoverable.Use hysteresis: do not flip a behavior hypothesis after a single contradictory action.Cap adaptation so a player who becomes unpredictable is rewarded rather than punished indefinitely.Keep all adaptation local to the session unless persistence is deliberately added later.7. Functional requirements and acceptance criteriaIDRequirementAcceptance criteriaFR-01Start a session without sign-in.Player can click Play and enter Level 1 within one screen transition.FR-02Capture behavior events.Core actions generate timestamped in-memory events with no personally identifying data.FR-03Compute behavior profile.After enough evidence, current profile contains confidence scores for supported traits.FR-04Select adaptive modifiers.Levels 2-5 can consume profile traits and choose a matching counter.FR-05Provide counterplay.Each adaptive hazard has a viable alternate behavior or route.FR-06Show selective observations.At least three distinct observation messages can appear based on actual session evidence.FR-07Build the personal level.Level 5 consumes at least three high-confidence traits or fallback traits.FR-08Reset cleanly.Replay can reset the session and produce a new baseline without stale state leakage.FR-09Persist optional settings only.Mute/theme/settings may persist locally; behavior can be session-only by default.FR-10Run offline after load.Core gameplay continues if network connectivity drops after initial page load.Definition of done for the MVPOne complete 5-level run is playable from landing page to ending without manual intervention.At least three player habits can materially change later hazards.At least one reveal message can be traced directly to real session evidence.The final level can be solved by changing strategy; brute-force random repetition is not the intended solution.No critical console errors during a normal run.Production build completes on Vercel and the game loads within an acceptable first-visit budget.Competition submission assets include the actual prompt log used during development.9.
